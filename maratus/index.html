maratus — three directions

Three complete single-page sites, built from one fact sheet (content.md) and sharing no stylesheet, font stack, palette or script. Same claims, same section order, three ways of telling it. Nothing here is published: every page is noindex and the live site is elsewhere.

  1. Direction A

    The Display

    Takes the display literally. Deep neutral ground, editorial type, and iridescence treated as a material rather than a background wash; the spider is drawn once, as a natural-history specimen plate, and raises its fan as you read the sequence.

    Risk the identity rests on one drawing and one scroll-driven motion. If either fails to land, what is left is well-set editorial type that any good studio could also produce.

  2. Direction B

    Maratus

    Character led. An illustrated spider with three working states — idle, displaying, paid — guides the page, in a warmer palette with softer type and spring-based micro-interactions.

    Risk a character can read as toy-like for counter hardware sold to shop owners. Building it is how we find out whether that risk is real.

  3. Direction C

    Structural colour

    No figure at all. The identity is optics — a generated interference field that shifts with scroll and cursor — set on a severe grid with monospaced data, dark and high contrast.

    Risk at rest, the optics stop arguing, so the page has to hold as a grid of type alone; and it can read cold to a shop owner even while it reads right to a developer.

Judge each one three times over: at 390, 768 and 1440 pixels wide, with reduced motion on, and with JavaScript off. A direction that only works in the first of those is not finished. The written pass is in REVIEW.md.
