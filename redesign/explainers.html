three ways to tell the same true thing

Same facts.
Three tellings.

One is a scene, one is a clock you can break on purpose, one is a sentence that rewrites itself. Each foregrounds a different truth — what it feels like at the counter, when you actually pay, and what we can and cannot see.

a · the counter

Six seconds at a till.

The literal version: a counter seen from the side, one sale, one handover. It foregrounds what it feels like — including the part nobody animates, where the box quietly goes back to your screen.

  1. The sale closes.
  2. The till sends the address — up, and back down to that one box.
  3. The code appears.
  4. They scan. The page loads from you, not from us.
  5. And the box goes back to being your screen.
b · the clock

Break it on purpose.

The money version, and the only one you can sabotage. Run it clean, then unplug the box and watch the credit come back — that is the fact a shopkeeper actually wants proven, not asserted.

  1. One credit goes on hold. Not spent — held.
  2. The box shows the code and says so.
  3. That answer is what spends it.
  4. No answer. The window runs out and the hold comes back, whole.
c · the sentence

One sentence, rewriting itself.

No diagram at all. The explanation is a line of type that keeps replacing its own last word, and the word it replaces turns into the thing it names.

Your till sends a link. We put a code on the counter. They point a camera at it. Your page opens on their phone. You paid for one showing.

Nothing else moves. Each line replaces the one before it, and the noun is the only word that ever carries a picture — because the noun is the only thing that changes in the product.
